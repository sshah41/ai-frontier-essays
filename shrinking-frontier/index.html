27 billion parameters

In August, Alibaba released Qwen3.8 27B. It scores 33.7 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, more than any model of any size released before OpenAI's GPT-5.4 on 5 March. Five months after the frontier reached that level, it fits on a single consumer graphics card.

The leading models today are vast. Kimi K3 has 2.8 trillion parameters, and the proprietary leaders do not publish theirs. What is changing fastest is how quickly their abilities reach models a hundred times smaller.

What “small” means here

Artificial Analysis sorts open-weights models into size classes by total parameter count. This piece uses two of them as stand-ins for consumer hardware. The conversions to memory below are rough rules of thumb for 4-bit quantized weights, about half a byte per parameter plus overhead.

The middle class is the odd one out. The best model between 40 and 150 billion parameters, NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, scores 26.5, below the best 27B model. Labs have put their effort into two shapes: very large mixture-of-experts models for data centres, and compact dense models for single machines.

The laptop line

Models with 40 billion parameters or fewer have followed the frontier at a fairly steady distance of five to nine months through 2026. Qwen3.5 27B, in February, came within a tenth of a point of GPT-5 from the previous August. Qwen3.8 27B, in August, passed every model released before GPT-5.4 in March. The frontier rose about 18 points over those six months, so the small models had to climb almost as fast just to stay the same distance behind.

A model that fits on one graphics card now scores what no model of any size could score in February.

Speed matters on a laptop, and mixture-of-experts designs help there too. Qwen3.6 35B A3B and GLM-4.7-Flash keep only about 3 billion parameters active per token, so they generate text quickly on modest hardware, at some cost in score.

The phone line

The smallest class moves more slowly but has had its biggest jump this autumn. K2 Horizon 3.7B, released in September by the UAE's Institute of Foundation Models, scores 15.6. That beats o1, the frontier of December 2024, and every model released before Claude 3.7 Sonnet in February 2025. Twenty-one months is a long lag by today's standards, but in 2024 the best phone-size models were roughly at the level of the original GPT-4.

Price is the other road to consumers

Not every consumer will run a model locally. For most, frontier ability arrives as a cheaper API model inside an app. On that road the decline has been steeper still.

The first model to score 15, o1 in December 2024, cost $26.25 per million blended tokens. By April 2026, Tencent's Hy3-preview reached the same score for about 10 cents, roughly 260 times less. The first model to pass 40, Claude Opus 4.7 in April 2026, cost $10. Claude Haiku 5.5 passed 40 in October at 20 cents. Price per token is not the whole cost: reasoning models that think longer use more tokens per task, so per-task savings are usually smaller than these ratios.

What it means

For people, capable assistants that run entirely on a personal device stop being a compromise. A laptop model at today's small-model level would have been the best in the world in February. That makes private, offline and free-to-run use realistic for work that used to require a frontier API.

For app builders, the sensible design is a tiered one: a small local or cheap model for most requests, with escalation to a frontier model for the hard ones. The tier boundaries move every few months, so routing logic should be easy to retune.

For the leading labs, the window in which a capability is exclusive keeps shrinking, and not only against rival frontier labs. Within half a year, the same ability shows up in a download that anyone can run.

For policy, small open models are the hardest to monitor or restrict once released. Some argue this calls for safeguards before release of capable open weights; others argue that local models improve privacy and resilience and that restrictions would mostly hand an advantage to closed providers. The trend makes the question more pressing either way.

Caveats

  • Artificial Analysis tests models at full precision on capable servers. Quantized versions on consumer hardware usually score somewhat lower.
  • Small reasoning models often think for a long time. On a laptop that can mean slow answers even when the final score is high.
  • Parameter counts are only known for open-weights models. Proprietary small models such as Claude Haiku 5.5 appear in the price chart but not in the size classes.
  • The index measures performance on a fixed set of tests, not every task people care about.

How this was made

Scores, release dates, parameter counts, size classes and prices come from model data on Artificial Analysis model pages, read on 23 October 2026: 697 models released since January 2023, 396 of them with published parameter counts. “Passes everything before” names the first model of any size that scored at least as high. Size classes follow Artificial Analysis: tiny up to about 4.5B parameters, small up to 40B, medium up to 150B, large above that.
