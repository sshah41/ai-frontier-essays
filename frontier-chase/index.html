Two and a half points

At some point today, Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro, and the distance between the best American model and the best Chinese model fell to 2.5 points on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Measured as a share of the leader's score, China has never been closer.

Claude Opus 5.5, released by Anthropic on 22 September, still holds the top score at 57.6. Kimi K3.1 Pro scores 55.1. A month ago the gap between the two countries' best models was 11.3 points. One release closed most of it.

Three years on one scale

Artificial Analysis rescores old models whenever it changes its index, so every model in this dataset sits on the same scale. That scale is hard. Version 4.3, introduced in September, weights agentic work at 30 percent and draws on tests such as Terminal-Bench 4.0, AutomationBench and Humanity's Last Exam. On it, GPT-4 scores 6.7.

The shape that comes out is a staircase that climbs slowly, then fast. From GPT-4 in March 2023 to o1 in December 2024, the frontier rose from 6.7 to 15.2. In the 22 months since, it has climbed to 57.6, nearly four times as high. Most of the climb has come in 2026, with eight new frontier records this year alone.

The race between countries

The United States has held the top score on every day in the dataset. China's position behind it has swung widely. When DeepSeek R1 arrived in January 2025 it cut the distance to o1 from 6.7 points to 3.8. Kimi K2 Thinking narrowed it to 2.9 in November 2025. Then a run of American releases (GPT-5.4 in March, Claude Fable 5 in June) pushed the gap to 19.2 points, the widest it has been.

Since then the gap has been closing faster than at any time in the record. Kimi K3 in July brought China back to within 6 points. Today's release takes it to 2.5. Press play above with the US vs China race selected to watch the whole sequence.

A month ago the gap was 11.3 points. One release closed most of it.

There is a twist in how China got here. Kimi K3, Moonshot's July flagship, shipped with open weights. Kimi K3.1 Pro did not; Artificial Analysis lists it as proprietary. It fits a wider pattern in the data. Chinese labs accounted for one proprietary scored model in 2024, seven in 2025 and 24 so far in 2026.

Open versus closed: a gap in points, a sprint in time

The open-weights story looks different depending on how you measure it. In points, the best closed model leads the best open-weights model, Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro, by 11.3. That is wider than a year ago, when the gap was 6.4. The low point was 2.9, in November 2025.

Measured in time, open weights are catching up faster every year. The chart below takes every model that set a new open-weights high and asks how long ago a proprietary model first reached the same score. In 2024 the answer averaged 7.8 months. In 2025 it was 5.9 months. In 2026 it is 3.1 months, and Kimi K3 needed only about five weeks to beat every closed model released before 9 June.

Both readings are true at once. The closed frontier keeps moving, so a fixed lag in time still shows up as a large gap in points whenever the frontier jumps. When frontier progress is this fast, three months behind can mean ten points behind.

Why the lag keeps shrinking

The index does not explain causes, but the records point to a few likely contributors.

  • Reasoning went public. Before January 2025 no open model had matched a closed reasoning model. DeepSeek R1 matched o1 six weeks after o1's release and published its methods. Every open record since has been a reasoning model.
  • Mixture-of-experts at huge scale. The leading open models are very large in total but use a small slice per token: Kimi K3 has 2.8 trillion parameters with 104 billion active, and MiMo-V2.6-Pro has 1.02 trillion with 42 billion active. That keeps the cost of serving them manageable.
  • Volume. China-based labs released 90 open-weights model variants that Artificial Analysis scored in 2026, against 38 from US labs. More attempts mean more chances to set a record.

What it means

For builders, leadership is now measured in weeks and changes hands with single releases. A model chosen in September can trail by a wide margin in October, and the reverse. Contracts, routing and evaluation pipelines that make switching easy are worth more than a bet on any one lab.

For the leading labs, a lead buys a few months of advantage, not a year. The value of being first depends more and more on what is built around a model (products, tools, distribution) and less on the score itself.

For policy, the data will be read in different ways. Supporters of tighter export controls on chips and models argue that the 2.5-point gap shows controls have not yet bitten and need strengthening. Critics argue that China closed the distance despite controls, and that restrictions mainly push talent and investment elsewhere. The index measures capability on a set of tests; it does not settle that argument.

For everyone else, frontier-level capability now reaches cheaper and smaller models within months. The companion piece below follows that story down to models that fit on a laptop or a phone.

What to watch

  • Whether the US labs answer Kimi K3.1 Pro within weeks, as they have after every previous narrowing.
  • Whether Moonshot and other Chinese labs keep their best models closed. If they do, the open-weights line could fall further behind the frontier even as China gains.
  • American open weights. Meta's Muse Spark Open (38.9, 15 October) is the strongest US open model to date, and Reflection's Beam (30) is second.
  • Intelligence Index v5. Artificial Analysis is rolling out harder evaluations, and each change of scale has widened the measured gaps.

How this was made

Scores, release dates, licences and creator countries come from the model data embedded in Artificial Analysis model pages, read on 23 October 2026 (697 models released since January 2023). Where several variants of a model share a day, the highest-scoring one counts. Artificial Analysis marks many older scores as estimates; the tooltips say which. Earlier index versions produced smaller gaps: Artificial Analysis reported a 4-point open-versus-closed gap at Kimi K3's launch, on version 4.2.
